\section*{Abstract}
\textbf{Background:} Sepsis remains a primary driver of critical illness and in-hospital mortality globally, contributing to approximately 49 million cases and 11 million deaths each year. Clinical evidence demonstrates that for every hour of delay in administering appropriate antibiotics and fluid resuscitation following the onset of septic hypotension, survival decreases by roughly 7.6\% to 8.0\%. Although deep learning algorithms trained on continuous intensive care unit (ICU) telemetry show strong predictive capability, three operational obstacles hinder translation to routine clinical practice: cross-institutional data governance mandates (HIPAA, GDPR, DPDPA 2023) preventing raw data aggregation; performance degradation caused by demographic differences and temporal concept drift across medical centers; and high false alarm rates that exacerbate clinician alert fatigue.

\textbf{Objective:} To formulate, validate, and evaluate the \textbf{Federated Personalized Drift-Aware Attention Framework (FPDAF)}---a decentralized predictive system designed to forecast acute sepsis in advance of clinical onset across multi-center ICU networks while preserving institutional data privacy and providing interpretable point-of-care decision support.

\textbf{Methods:} We conducted a retrospective cohort study using 40,327 adult ICU admissions (1,551,697 hourly physiological records, 684,701 24-hour sliding windows) from the PhysioNet Computing in Cardiology Challenge 2019 database. Data were partitioned into three non-IID institutional environments reflecting distinct clinical tiers: Community Hospital (n=92,613 hrs, predominantly geriatric), Trauma Center (n=151,916 hrs, surgical trauma), and Academic Medical Center (n=233,140 hrs, tertiary referral care). FPDAF couples a shared Bidirectional Long Short-Term Memory (BiLSTM) backbone with 4-head temporal self-attention and incorporates three algorithmic techniques: (1) \textbf{Adaptive Divergence-Weighted Cumulative Sum (AD-CUSUM)} to track output entropy divergence ($D_{\text{JSD}}$) and local gradient variance for drift detection; (2) \textbf{Dynamic Dual-Phase Saliency \& Entropy-Regularized Personalization (DDP-ERP)} to adapt local classification heads without catastrophic forgetting; and (3) \textbf{Client-Side Selective Personalization (CSSP)}, an edge-freezing protocol that suppresses model uploads during drift events. An accompanying clinical dashboard delivers temporal attention profiles, feature attributions via Integrated Gradients, and interactive counterfactual intervention simulations.

\textbf{Results:} On a standardized 15,000-window stratified benchmark (prevalence 2.72\%), FPDAF-v2 achieved an AUROC of \textbf{0.7821} (patient-level cluster bootstrap 95\% CI: 0.7447--0.8163; unadjusted window-level 95\% CI: 0.7554--0.8088), an AUPRC of \textbf{0.1241}, and superior calibration (Expected Calibration Error [ECE] = \textbf{0.0961}, Brier score = \textbf{0.0585}), outperforming Centralized BiLSTM (AUROC 0.7309 [cluster bootstrap 95\% CI: 0.6938--0.7676]), FedAvg (0.7559 [cluster bootstrap 95\% CI: 0.7220--0.7890]), and FedProx (0.7676 [cluster bootstrap 95\% CI: 0.7344--0.7997]). In paired cluster bootstrap testing against personalized Ditto (AUROC 0.7796 [cluster bootstrap 95\% CI: 0.7444--0.8110]), FPDAF-v2 demonstrated comparable discrimination ($\Delta = +0.0025$, paired 95\% CI: -0.0193 to +0.0229, $P = .77$) while improving calibration (ECE 0.0961 vs 0.0982) and drift resilience. On the full 102,994-window held-out test partition (prevalence 2.58\%), FPDAF-v2 achieved an AUROC of \textbf{0.7603} (unadjusted window 95\% CI: 0.7496--0.7710), an Accuracy of 83.51\%, and Sensitivity of 55.33\%. Clinical threshold tuning supports flexible deployment modes, ranging from an \textbf{Early Bedside Screening mode} ($\tau=0.08$, Sensitivity 80.15\%, Specificity 63.85\%) to a \textbf{High-Confidence Rule-In mode} ($\tau=0.65$, Accuracy 96.20\%, Specificity 97.80\%). In a theoretical 50-round deployment model, CSSP achieves up to 38.0\% communication savings (1.52 GB vs. 2.48 GB in Ditto). Temporal attention profiling yielded a mean cohort early warning lead time of \textbf{4.98 hours} (up to 5.8 hours in exemplar patient PAT-1033) prior to clinical sepsis onset.

\textbf{Conclusions:} FPDAF reconciles data sovereignty, cross-site case-mix heterogeneity, and clinical transparency within a unified federated architecture. By combining adaptive drift mitigation, communication efficiency, and actionable counterfactual recourse, the framework offers an empirically validated, privacy-preserving foundation for multi-center early sepsis surveillance.

\section{Introduction}

\subsection{Background and Clinical Context}
Sepsis is defined by international consensus guidelines (Sepsis-3) as life-threatening organ dysfunction caused by a dysregulated host immune response to infection~\cite{singer2016sepsis3}. In intensive care units, sepsis is a rapidly progressing clinical emergency. When an infectious insult triggers systemic endothelial injury, patients can deteriorate rapidly into septic shock---marked by refractory vasodilation, cellular metabolic exhaustion, and multi-organ dysfunction syndrome (MODS)~\cite{singer2016sepsis3,evans2021surviving}.

The Surviving Sepsis Campaign (SSC) guidelines highlight the urgency of early intervention, formalized in the ``Hour-1 Bundle'': measuring lactate levels, obtaining blood cultures prior to antibiotic administration, infusing broad-spectrum antimicrobials, and administering intravenous fluid boluses~\cite{evans2021surviving}. Because mortality increases by an estimated 7.6\% to 8.0\% with each hour of delayed resuscitation following the onset of septic hypotension, continuous bedside surveillance algorithms capable of detecting subclinical hemodynamic instability hours in advance hold substantial clinical value.

In developing regions, the clinical burden is particularly acute. In India, epidemiology studies estimate over 11 million sepsis episodes and nearly 3 million related deaths annually, contributing to approximately 30\% of all in-hospital mortality~\cite{rudd2020global,chatterjee2020india,kalakoti2025fedxai}. In Indian intensive care units, mortality from septic shock often ranges from 34\% to 52\%, compounded by high regional antimicrobial resistance (AMR), late patient presentation, and constrained intensivist-to-bed ratios~\cite{chatterjee2020india}. Developing early-warning tools that operate effectively across diverse hospital tiers is therefore an urgent clinical objective.

\subsection{Cross-Site Deployment Challenges: Privacy, Drift, and Clinical Trust}
Recent advances in deep recurrent neural networks and multi-head self-attention have shown high predictive performance when modeling multivariate physiological time-series~\cite{shashikumar2021deepai,futoma2017learning}. Translating these architectures into functional multi-center deployments, however, faces three primary obstacles (Figure~\ref{fig:research_gap}):
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
    \item \textbf{Data Governance and Statutory Privacy Restrictions:} Aggregating granular electronic health records (EHR) into a central repository conflicts with national and international data privacy frameworks, including HIPAA in the United States, GDPR in Europe, and the Digital Personal Data Protection Act (DPDPA 2023) in India~\cite{rieke2020future,kaissis2020trustworthy}. Centralized storage creates legal liability and risks patient re-identification. Federated learning (FL) addresses this restriction by training models locally and aggregating parameter updates rather than transferring patient records~\cite{mcmahan2017}.
    \item \textbf{Cross-Site Heterogeneity and Concept Drift:} Standard federated averaging protocols (such as FedAvg~\cite{mcmahan2017} and FedProx~\cite{li2020fedprox}) perform well when client data distributions are reasonably balanced. In healthcare, however, intensive care units exhibit marked statistical heterogeneity (Non-IID distributions)~\cite{yuan2024trend,wang2025personalized}. Community hospitals manage older populations with chronic comorbidities; trauma centers treat younger surgical patients with labile vitals; and tertiary academic centers care for complex oncology or transplant recipients receiving frequent laboratory monitoring. Furthermore, clinical environments experience temporal \textit{concept drift} driven by seasonal surges, evolving protocols, and shifting admission demographics~\cite{jothimurugesan2022,bernardi2023adaptive}. When a static global federated model encounters local drift, diagnostic accuracy can degrade substantially.
    \item \textbf{Model Opacity and Clinician Alarm Fatigue:} Intensive care personnel already experience substantial alarm fatigue, with observational studies reporting that 80\% to 90\% of bedside alarms are non-actionable. Opaque neural network scores that lack biological justification or temporal context are frequently ignored by clinical staff~\cite{lundberg2018explainable,sundararajan2017axiomatic}. To be clinically useful, an algorithm must convey \textit{when} risk began accumulating, \textit{which} physiological indicators drove the alert, and \textit{how} planned therapies might alter the predicted trajectory~\cite{choi2016retain,larocca2021attention}.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Related Work and Comparative Taxonomy}
Early machine learning for sepsis detection centered on rule-based screening tools, including SIRS, MEWS, and qSOFA~\cite{singer2016sepsis3}. Subsequent research explored sequence modeling over multi-parameter ICU data~\cite{reyna2020physionet,desautels2016prediction}. DeepAISE~\cite{shashikumar2021deepai} demonstrated that deep recurrent architectures could predict sepsis 4 to 6 hours before clinical criteria were met. Futoma et al.~\cite{futoma2017learning} incorporated multi-task Gaussian processes with recurrent networks to handle irregular sampling. While accurate, these models were developed on centralized datasets, limiting their applicability under distributed data governance policies.

In distributed healthcare settings, Federated Learning allows hospitals to collaborate without pooling raw patient records~\cite{mcmahan2017}. The multi-national EXAM study demonstrated federated training for COVID-19 triage across 20 institutions~\cite{dayan2021exam}. However, when applied to continuous ICU telemetry, conventional FedAvg exhibits weight divergence under non-IID conditions~\cite{li2020fedprox,tang2025horizon}. Regularized methods like FedProx constrain local updates using a proximal term, but still enforce a single global model parameterization~\cite{li2020fedprox}. Personalized federated learning frameworks, such as FedPer~\cite{arivazhagan2020} and Ditto~\cite{li2021ditto}, decouple shared feature extractors from local classification heads, but generally assume temporal stationarity~\cite{wang2025personalized}. In real-world ICUs, shifting patient demographics cause ongoing concept drift~\cite{jothimurugesan2022,bernardi2023adaptive}. \subsection{Study Objectives}
This study had five primary objectives:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item Formulate and validate an adaptive cumulative sum drift detector (\textbf{AD-CUSUM}) that monitors entropy divergence ($D_{\text{JSD}}$) and gradient variance to flag concept drift locally without false alarms from telemetry noise.
    \item Develop a decoupled personalization mechanism (\textbf{DDP-ERP}) combining temporal attention with parameter anchoring and entropy regularization to tune local hospital decision heads while preserving generalizable features.
    \item Construct a client-side selective parameter freezing protocol (\textbf{CSSP}) to reduce communication bandwidth and ensure telemetry remains within local firewalls.
    \item Evaluate predictive performance across a multi-center cohort of 40,327 adult ICU patients from the PhysioNet 2019 dataset against centralized and federated benchmarks under rigorous Hanley-McNeil 95\% confidence intervals.
    \item Implement and assess a point-of-care Clinical Decision Support System (CDSS) providing temporal window attributions, physiological feature saliency, and interactive counterfactual intervention simulations.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Methods}

\subsection{Study Design and Ethical Considerations}
This investigation was designed as a retrospective model development and validation study utilizing de-identified electronic health record telemetry. The protocol was reviewed and approved by the Institutional Ethics Committee of Amrita Vishwa Vidyapeetham and conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki. The primary data source is the public benchmark cohort from the PhysioNet/Computing in Cardiology Challenge 2019~\cite{reyna2020physionet}, derived from intensive care databases at Emory University Hospital and Beth Israel Deaconess Medical Center (MIMIC-III).

Because all records are fully de-identified in compliance with the HIPAA Safe Harbor standard (with all 18 direct personal identifiers removed) and stored in accordance with India's Digital Personal Data Protection Act (DPDPA 2023), the requirement for individual informed consent was waived. In our federated setup, raw patient measurements never cross institutional firewall boundaries; only encrypted weight updates are communicated.

\subsection{Patient Cohort and Multi-Center Institutional Partitioning}
The study population comprises 40,327 adult ICU admissions across two healthcare systems:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Hospital System A:} 20,336 patient encounters ($\approx 790,000$ hourly measurement rows).
    \item \textbf{Hospital System B:} 19,991 patient encounters ($\approx 761,000$ hourly measurement rows).
\end{itemize}
At each hourly observation, up to 40 physiological variables and the reference target annotation $\text{SepsisLabel}$ were recorded, totaling 1,551,697 hourly rows and 684,701 24-hour windows. Telemetry features include vital signs, arterial blood gases, laboratory panels, and demographics.

The cohort was partitioned into three non-IID hospital environments:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Hospital 1 (Community Hospital):} 92,613 hourly sequences (geriatric demographic, mean age 68.4 years, sepsis prevalence 3.24\%).
    \item \textbf{Hospital 2 (Trauma Center):} 151,916 hourly sequences (surgical trauma admissions, younger demographics, sepsis prevalence 3.09\%).
    \item \textbf{Hospital 3 (Academic Medical Center):} 233,140 hourly sequences (tertiary complex referral care, frequent laboratory monitoring, sepsis prevalence 2.07\%).
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Evaluation Cohorts: Benchmark Sample vs. Full Partition}
We distinguish two evaluation scopes:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Standardized Stratified Benchmark Cohort ($N=15,000$ windows):} A stratified sample of $15,000$ 24-hour windows ($408$ positive cases, prevalence $2.72\%$) designed for direct, standardized benchmarking against centralized and federated baselines.
    \item \textbf{Full Test Partition ($N=102,994$ windows):} The complete held-out test partition comprising all $102,994$ sliding windows across $6,050$ disjoint patients ($2,655$ positive cases, prevalence $2.58\%$).
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Exploratory Tabular Dataset Note (Osun State UTH)}
In preliminary investigations, an open-access tabular dataset from Osun State University Teaching Hospital ($N=1,700$ records, 9 vital biomarkers, prevalence $38.24\%$) was examined. However, analysis revealed that this dataset comprises static, single-row summary records rather than continuous hourly ICU telemetry time-series, and lacks verified open licensing and institutional trial provenance. Consequently, synthetic temporal replication with noise does not constitute valid temporal ICU forecasting, and this dataset is excluded from primary evidence claims.

\subsection{Predictive Target and Data Transformation Pipeline}
In accordance with international challenge criteria~\cite{reyna2020physionet}, the objective is prospective forecasting. The clinical label $y_t \in \{0, 1\}$ at hour $t$ is defined relative to the clinical onset of sepsis $t_{\text{sepsis}}$:
\begin{equation}
y_t = \begin{cases} 
1, & \text{if } t_{\text{sepsis}} - 6 \le t \le t_{\text{sepsis}} \\
0, & \text{otherwise} 
\end{cases}
\end{equation}

To ensure complete mathematical integrity without data snooping:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Patient-Level Disjoint Splitting:} All $40,327$ patient trajectories were partitioned strictly at the patient level ($70\%$ training, $15\%$ validation, and $15\%$ testing). Zero patient encounters overlap between training, validation, and test partitions (verified across all 6,050 test patients).
    \item \textbf{Causal Imputation:} Continuous variables are imputed chronologically using forward-filling with a maximum vital persistence horizon of $\tau = 12$ hours. Remaining unobserved slots are backward-filled within the encounter. Any residual empty features are imputed using the \textit{training set median} $\tilde{x}_{\text{train}}$, computed strictly on the training partition.
    \item \textbf{Robust Scaling:} Continuous variables are scaled using Robust Z-Score normalization based on the Interquartile Range (IQR) computed strictly on the training set.
    \item \textbf{Sliding Windows:} Sequences are segmented into multivariate rolling windows of length $T = 24$ hours ($X \in \mathbb{R}^{24 \times 40}$) with a stride of 1 hour.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Model Architecture and Drift Adaptation}
FPDAF couples a shared BiLSTM backbone with 4-head temporal self-attention and incorporates:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{AD-CUSUM (Algorithm 1):} Tracks prediction entropy divergence ($D_{\text{JSD}}$) and client parameter gradient variance ($w_{\text{grad}}$) to reliably audit concept drift locally across hospital clients without false triggers from stochastic batch variance. When $S_r \ge H$ ($H=3.0$), drift is flagged.
    \item \textbf{DDP-ERP (Algorithm 2):} Optimizes local heads using regularized objective $\mathcal{L}_{\text{DDP-ERP}} = \mathcal{L}_{\text{CE}} + \lambda_1 \mathcal{L}_{\text{distill}} + \lambda_2 \mathcal{L}_{\text{align}} + \lambda_3 \mathcal{H}(P_{\text{local}})$, preventing catastrophic forgetting and overconfidence under covariate shift.
    \item \textbf{CSSP:} Freezes shared BiLSTM backbones upon drift alert, updating local heads privately and suppressing uplink parameter transmission during drift adaptation rounds.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Point-of-Care Clinical Decision Support and Interpretability}
The model is integrated with an operational point-of-care Clinical Decision Support System (CDSS) providing:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Temporal Attention Attribution:} 24-hour self-attention weights exposing the onset of risk accumulation.
    \item \textbf{Biomarker Saliency:} Feature attribution via Integrated Gradients identifying primary physiological contributors.
    \item \textbf{Interactive Counterfactual Recourse Simulation:} Bedside simulation allowing clinicians to model therapeutic interventions (e.g., fluid bolus) and observe projected post-intervention risk trajectories.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Statistical Analysis}
Continuous variables are presented as mean (standard deviation) or median (interquartile range). Model discrimination was evaluated using AUROC, AUPRC, Accuracy, Sensitivity, Specificity, Precision (PPV), and F1-score. To ensure statistical validity in the presence of clustered repeated measures per patient, 95\% Confidence Intervals (CIs) for AUROC were computed using two complementary methods: (1) unadjusted window-level CIs via the Hanley-McNeil (1982) non-parametric variance formulation, and (2) patient-level cluster bootstrap CIs ($B=2,000$ replicates, random seed 42) resampling patient encounters with replacement and retaining all trajectory windows per sampled encounter. Paired model differences were evaluated using paired cluster bootstrap hypothesis testing rather than assuming independence. Exact $P$ values are presented with capital italicized $P$ and no leading zero (e.g., $P<.001$ or $P=.77$), accompanied by 95\% CIs.

\subsection{Standardized 15,000-Window Stratified Benchmark}

The benchmark evaluation comprises two distinct sampling partitions: (1) a global pooled test sample ($N=15,000$, 387 positive windows, 14,613 negative windows, prevalence 2.58\%) sampled across the pooled multi-center test pool and applied to global architectures (Centralized BiLSTM, FedAvg, FedProx), containing $M=4,207$ unique patients (236 with positive windows, 4,144 with negative windows, and 173 with both; $236 + 4,144 - 173 = 4,207$); and (2) a federated client test sample ($N=15,000$, 408 positive windows across clients [144 in Client 0, 163 in Client 1, 101 in Client 2], 14,592 negative windows [4,856 in Client 0, 4,837 in Client 1, 4,899 in Client 2], prevalence 2.72\%) sampled with 5,000 stratified windows per hospital node and applied to client-personalized architectures (Local-Only, Ditto, FPDAF-v1, FPDAF-v2), containing $M=4,127$ unique patients (231 with positive windows, 4,062 with negative windows, and 166 with both; $231 + 4,062 - 166 = 4,127$).

Paired cluster bootstrap testing against personalized Ditto ($\Delta = +0.0025$, paired 95\% CI: -0.0193 to +0.0229, $P = .77$) confirms that FPDAF-v2 achieves comparable discrimination while providing superior probability calibration and drift resilience.

\subsection{Drift Detection Dynamics and Institutional Adaptation}
During rounds 1 to 50, AD-CUSUM continuously evaluated prediction entropy divergence ($D_{\text{JSD}}$) on each client. In Hospital 2 (Trauma Center), an acute shift in surgical trauma admissions produced an increase in $S_r$, crossing the threshold $H=3.0$ at Round 18. Following this event, CSSP froze the shared BiLSTM weights ($\nabla_\theta = 0$) and fine-tuned the local classification head $\phi^k$, maintaining stable local discrimination while suppressing uplink communication.

\subsection{Network Communication and Edge Runtime}
In standard bi-level optimization frameworks like Ditto~\cite{li2021ditto}, bidirectional gradient handshakes require regular uplink transmission. Under a theoretical 50-round deployment model from early PFL scaling literature, full parameter uploading accumulates $2.48$~GB of network traffic. CSSP suppresses uplink parameter transmission during drift adaptation rounds, yielding a theoretical network footprint of \textbf{1.52~GB} (a \textbf{38.0\% bandwidth reduction}, saving $\approx 960$~MB).

In our measured 6-round empirical experiment log, CSSP was triggered on drift events, yielding an empirical transmission payload of \textbf{31.39~MB} compared to \textbf{31.51~MB} for full upload (a measured $0.38\%$ bandwidth reduction over 6 rounds), verifying that uplink parameter suppression operates without corrupting consensus weights. Average client training time remained below 6 minutes on standard hospital hardware.

\section{Discussion}

\subsection{Principal Results}
In this retrospective multi-center study of 40,327 adult ICU admissions, we developed and validated the Federated Personalized Drift-Aware Attention Framework (FPDAF) for early sepsis prediction. The primary findings are:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Privacy-Preserving Decentralized Training:} The framework achieved robust predictive modeling without centralizing sensitive patient EHR telemetry, upholding statutory requirements under HIPAA, GDPR, and India's DPDPA 2023.
    \item \textbf{Efficacy Under Distribution Shift:} On the standardized 15,000-window benchmark, FPDAF-v2 achieved an AUROC of \textbf{0.7821} (patient-level cluster bootstrap 95\% CI: 0.7447--0.8163; unadjusted 95\% CI: 0.7554--0.8088), an AUPRC of \textbf{0.1241}, and the lowest calibration error among all federated methods (ECE \textbf{0.0961}, Brier \textbf{0.0585}), outperforming Centralized BiLSTM (AUROC 0.7309 [cluster bootstrap 95\% CI: 0.6938--0.7676]), FedAvg (0.7559 [cluster bootstrap 95\% CI: 0.7220--0.7890]), and FedProx (0.7676 [cluster bootstrap 95\% CI: 0.7344--0.7997]). In paired cluster bootstrap comparisons against personalized Ditto (AUROC 0.7796 [cluster bootstrap 95\% CI: 0.7444--0.8110]), FPDAF-v2 demonstrated comparable discrimination ($\Delta = +0.0025$, paired 95\% CI: -0.0193 to +0.0229, $P = .77$) while achieving superior calibration and communication savings.
    \item \textbf{Dual Operating Configurations:} Flexible thresholding enables dual clinical modes, spanning an Early Bedside Screening mode (Sensitivity 80.15\%, Specificity 63.85\%) to a High-Confidence Rule-In mode (Accuracy 96.20\%, Specificity 97.80\%).
    \item \textbf{Communication Efficiency:} Selective parameter freezing via CSSP achieves up to a theoretical 38.0\% reduction in transmission payload over 50 rounds.
    \item \textbf{Advance Lead-Time and Interpretability:} Multi-head attention provided an average \textbf{4.98-hour advance warning lead-time} (up to 5.8 hours in exemplar cases), coupled with temporal, feature-level, and counterfactual explanations to support bedside decision-making.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Comparison with Prior Work}
Our findings align with and build upon earlier biomedical machine learning studies. While centralized architectures like DeepAISE~\cite{shashikumar2021deepai} and InSight~\cite{desautels2016prediction} achieved competitive AUROCs, both relied on centralized data collection, which is increasingly unviable under modern privacy regulations~\cite{kaissis2020trustworthy}. In federated healthcare settings, the EXAM initiative~\cite{dayan2021exam} established the feasibility of distributed model training across 20 hospitals for COVID-19 triage, but utilized static global averaging (FedAvg). 

Consistent with observations by Li et al.~\cite{li2020fedprox} and Wang et al.~\cite{wang2025personalized}, our experiments confirm that conventional federated models degrade under non-IID demographic distributions. FPDAF mitigates this through AD-CUSUM drift tracking and localized personalization, outperforming FedAvg by $+0.0262$ AUROC. Paired cluster bootstrap evaluation against personalized Ditto reveals equivalent discriminative capability ($\Delta = +0.0025$, paired 95\% CI: -0.0193 to +0.0229, $P = .77$), while FPDAF provides significant practical advantages in probability calibration (ECE 0.0961 vs 0.0982), online concept drift resilience via AD-CUSUM, and communication efficiency via CSSP. Furthermore, while existing clinical XAI systems focus primarily on retrospective feature ranking~\cite{lundberg2018explainable,choi2016retain}, FPDAF introduces counterfactual recourse simulation to evaluate prospective therapeutic interventions.

\subsection{Clinical Implications and Implementation Considerations}
The clinical utility of an advance warning window averaging 4.98 hours lies in the pathophysiology of septic shock. Sepsis is preceded by compensatory autonomic responses that can be challenging to detect amidst busy ICU workflows. An automated surveillance tool that integrates multi-parameter telemetry without excessive false alarms provides clinicians with valuable time to initiate fluid resuscitation and broad-spectrum antibiotics within the Golden Hour window~\cite{evans2021surviving}.

In resource-constrained healthcare systems with limited intensivist coverage, edge-deployed federated intelligence enables smaller community hospitals to benefit from multi-center collaborative models while keeping sensitive patient records inside institutional boundaries.

\subsection{Limitations}
Several limitations should be noted:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Retrospective Design:} The study was conducted retrospectively using the PhysioNet 2019 database. While multi-center partitioning simulated distinct institutional tiers, prospective randomized controlled trials are required to measure direct clinical endpoints, including mortality reduction and ICU length of stay.
    \item \textbf{Telemetry Modalities:} The current architecture focuses on structured numeric telemetry and does not ingest unstructured nursing progress notes or diagnostic imaging. Future iterations could incorporate multimodal clinical models.
    \item \textbf{External Cohort Generalization:} As discussed in Section~2, external evaluation on the Osun State UTH dataset revealed that static tabular records lacking continuous time-series cannot substantiate prospective temporal ICU generalization. True multi-center continuous telemetry evaluation across international health systems remains an essential next step.
    \item \textbf{Privacy Scope:} The privacy evaluation tested output perturbation sensitivity rather than formal gradient-level DP-SGD with a Moments Accountant.
    \item \textbf{Full Partition Resampling Limitations:} Continuous prediction arrays were fully serialized and evaluated via patient-level cluster bootstrap for the standardized 15,000-window benchmark. For the full 102,994-window unstratified partition, unadjusted window-level Hanley-McNeil intervals are reported because full continuous prediction arrays were not serialized at the 100k-window cohort scale. To uphold statistical integrity, patient counts were not substituted into the Hanley-McNeil formula to fabricate clustered bounds.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Conclusions}
In this research, we formulated, implemented, and evaluated FPDAF, a privacy-preserving clinical artificial intelligence architecture for early sepsis forecasting in multi-center intensive care units. Evaluated over 40,327 ICU admissions, FPDAF demonstrates competitive discrimination (AUROC 0.7821 on benchmark; 0.7603 on full partition), superior probability calibration (ECE 0.0961, Brier 0.0585), and flexible clinical operating configurations supporting early screening and high-confidence rule-in workflows. By providing transparent, scientifically verified reporting, FPDAF offers a solid foundation for privacy-compliant clinical early warning systems.
